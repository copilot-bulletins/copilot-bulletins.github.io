🏆 Top 5 updates of the month

1. Prove Copilot's ROI to your board

The impact dashboard now includes a dedicated return-on-investment section, translating Copilot usage into business value leaders can present. It's the clearest bridge yet from adoption metrics to financial justification, helping you defend and expand your investment with data rather than anecdotes.

→ Changelog: Copilot impact dashboard adds a return on investment section

2. Enterprise-wide model governance matures

Global model policy is now generally available, letting enterprises centrally standardize which AI models teams can use. Paired with an expanding lineup—Grok 4.6, Gemini 3.7 Flash, and Kimi K3—leaders get choice without losing control, balancing innovation with compliance and cost predictability.

Sources:
→ Changelog: Global model policy generally available

3. Copilot code review keeps compounding

Code review gained resolution reasons and expanded capabilities, while effort levels reached general availability. GitHub is investing steadily here because automated review scales quality and shortens cycle time—directly improving throughput and reducing the cost of defects across your engineering organization.

Sources:
→ Changelog: Copilot code review: Resolution reasons and expanded capabilities
→ Changelog: Copilot code review effort levels are generally available

4. Copilot meets teams where they collaborate

Shared agentic work arrived in Microsoft Teams alongside a new Copilot experience in Slack. Extending Copilot into everyday collaboration surfaces broadens reach beyond the IDE, driving adoption across roles and embedding AI assistance into the workflows your organization already runs on.

Sources:
→ Changelog: Shared agentic work with GitHub Copilot in Microsoft Teams

5. An extensible agentic platform takes shape

Agent Plugins 1.0 landed across VS Code, Copilot CLI, and the Copilot app, establishing a consistent way to extend Copilot's agentic capabilities. For decision-makers, this signals a maturing platform strategy—one you can build on, standardize, and grow with rather than a single-point tool.
